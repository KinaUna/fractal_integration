# The Human as the Outermost System

Everything we build lives inside a human life. The code lives inside a
developer's life. The product lives inside a user's life. The team lives
inside many lives. The human is the outermost system, and the integration
principles in this repo apply to it with the same rigor we apply to code —
because it is the system that contains all the others.

We do not treat well-being as soft. We treat it as the topmost scale of the
same principle: **quality is the quality of the linkage**, and the whole
(health, meaning, sustainability) is an emergent property that no single
part (output, hours, engagement) can be tuned to produce.

## Health and well-being are integration, not a part

- Health is not the absence of one illness (a part's metric). It is body,
  mind, and social life functioning as an integrated whole (an emergent
  property).
- Well-being is an emergent property of integrated sleep, movement,
  relationships, rest, and meaning. You can max out any single one and still
  be unwell, because the whole is the linkage, not the sum.
- **Burnout is an integration failure.** Work fragments the person; the
  parts stop coordinating; the whole degrades. It is the classic local
  optimization: a part (throughput) optimized at the cost of the whole (the
  person).

So the diagnosis we give a struggling system applies to a struggling person
too: *the parts are fine; the linkage is failing.*

## The developer is a system, and work is one of its parts

We are not resources that produce code. We are whole systems — body,
attention, relationships, purpose — for which software work is one
differentiated activity. That reframes three things:

**Boundaries are designed seams, not failures.**
A person with no boundary between work and life is a god module: everything
bleeds into everything, and the whole degrades. Rest, off-hours, and
non-work time are not outside the system. They are the feedback and
maintenance that let the whole sustain performance. Removing them is removing
the loop.

**Sustainability over spikes.**
A system that runs hot burns out. We design for the whole's sustainment, not
a part's peak. A pace that holds for a year beats a sprint that collapses the
person in a month.

**Meaning is integration.**
Work that links to a person's values and purpose sustains the whole. Work
that is fragmented busywork, or misaligned with what the person cares about,
drains it. Rituals that connect the work to the whole life are part of the
design, not perks.

## The product lives inside the user's life

[`product-as-integrator.md`](product-as-integrator.md) says the product's outbound integration flows
into the user's **attention, time, energy, relationships, and well-being** —
a *living life*, not just a next action. That raises the standard:

- An output that flows to a next action is good.
- An output that drains the life — attention, time, energy — is a leak.
  "Successful" and "integrated" are different: a notification that reliably
  creates urgency, or a streak that makes the user anxious to keep it, both
  "work" while colonizing the life.

This is why some products feel like a gift and others like a tax, even when
both "work." The difference is the integration with the life: one gives time
and attention back, the other colonizes them.

## Heuristics for the human system

- **The whole-person test:** does this work, goal, or feature optimize a part
  (output, engagement, velocity) at the cost of the whole (the person's
  health, life, meaning)?
- **Boundary check:** is the work-life boundary a designed seam with a
  contract, or a leak? Who owns it?
- **The time test:** does this give time and energy back, or take them?
- **Attention is a finite resource with a boundary:** every feature that takes
  the user's attention is crossing into their life-seam. Does it integrate
  with the life, or colonize it?
- **Sustainability check:** can this pace hold for a year? What does the whole
  pay for this peak?
- **Recovery is in the loop:** is rest, sleep, and downtime planned for as part
  of the system, or treated as absence?

## What we commit to

- We will not trade a person's whole for a part's output — for ourselves or
  for our users.
- Boundaries, rest, and recovery are first-class design artifacts, planned and
  reviewed like any other.
- When we measure success, we measure the whole (sustained, healthy,
  meaningful), not just a part (throughput, engagement, growth).
